Five decisions, try them yourself

Most of my week goes on deciding who to buy from, what price to accept, what to keep in stock and what to do when a shipment runs late. I turned five of those calls into small exercises you can try in a minute or two.

The numbers here are made up. The way of thinking is mine, but the details of what I do for my employer stay private.

The supplier showdown

The lowest quote often costs more by the time the part reaches the shelf.

Three suppliers have quoted for the same 1,000 brake pads. Pick one, then see what each would really cost once freight, duty and payment terms are counted.

Why it matters to you

A supplier with a low price can still be the expensive choice once freight and payment terms are added up. I compare the full cost before I recommend anyone.

The negotiation

Suppliers move on price when they get something useful in return.

You have four moves. Each one changes the price and how the supplier feels about you. Push too hard and they stop giving ground.

Why it matters to you

I try to offer a supplier something that helps them, such as steady volume or easier planning, in return for a better price. They remember it the next time we need a favour.

Run the quarter

When plans go wrong, fix what affects customers first and keep the response in proportion.

Three months and three surprises. Every choice moves cash, customers, margin and supplier goodwill, and none of the options is free.

Why it matters to you

When a shipment is late or costs change, I look at the effect on the whole business before acting. Spending heavily to fix a small problem is a mistake I work hard to avoid.
